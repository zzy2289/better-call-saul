# Landlord-Tenant Disputes

## Common dispute types

- Security deposit not returned or wrongful deductions.
- Uninhabitable conditions (mold, pests, broken heating/plumbing, safety hazards).
- Illegal rent increase.
- Wrongful eviction or eviction threats.
- Lease violations by landlord.
- Maintenance and repair refusal.
- Privacy violations (entering without notice).
- Utility billing disputes.
- Subletting or lease transfer conflicts.
- Move-out inspection disagreements.

## Core question

Does the landlord have a legal or contractual basis for their action, and has the tenant documented the dispute properly?

## Evidence checklist

- Lease agreement (full copy).
- Move-in and move-out inspection reports or photos/video.
- Written correspondence with landlord (emails, texts, letters).
- Receipts for repairs, cleaning, or improvements paid by tenant.
- Photos/video of property condition (timestamped).
- Rent payment records.
- Local housing code or tenant rights statute.
- Witness statements from neighbors or co-tenants.

## Key rights (jurisdiction-dependent)

### Security deposit

- Most jurisdictions require the landlord to return the deposit within a specified period (14-60 days depending on jurisdiction).
- Deductions must be itemized with receipts in many places.
- Normal wear and tear cannot be deducted.
- Some jurisdictions impose penalties (2x or 3x deposit) for wrongful withholding.

### Habitability

- Landlords must maintain the property in habitable condition.
- "Implied warranty of habitability" exists in most US states, UK, EU, Australia, and China.
- Tenants may have the right to "repair and deduct" or withhold rent in some jurisdictions (risky — verify local law first).

### Notice requirements

- Landlords must give proper notice before entering the property (typically 24-48 hours).
- Eviction requires proper legal process — "self-help" evictions (changing locks, removing belongings) are illegal almost everywhere.

## Escalation ladder

1. Written request to landlord (email, with timestamp and specific ask).
2. Follow-up with deadline and reference to lease terms.
3. Local tenant rights organization or housing authority.
4. Formal complaint to housing inspector or code enforcement.
5. Demand letter (certified mail or equivalent).
6. Mediation or tenant dispute board.
7. Small claims court or housing tribunal.

## Strong angles

### Deposit withholding

```text
I am requesting the return of my security deposit. The lease ended on [date] and I returned the keys on [date]. I have photos from move-in and move-out showing the property in the same condition minus normal wear and tear. Under [local law], the deposit must be returned within [X] days with an itemized statement of any deductions. Please provide the refund or a written, itemized explanation of deductions with receipts.
```

### Habitability

```text
I am writing to formally report [specific issue: mold, broken heating, pest infestation, etc.] in the property at [address]. This issue affects the habitability of the unit. I request that you arrange repairs within [reasonable timeframe]. Please respond in writing with a timeline for resolution. I have documented the current condition with photos/video dated [date].
```

### Illegal entry

```text
On [date], the property was entered without the required [X]-hour advance notice. The lease at Section [X] and [local law] require written notice before entry except in emergencies. Please ensure future entries comply with notice requirements.
```

## Saul angle

Landlord disputes are won by tenants who write things down. The moment you have a problem, switch every conversation to writing. Take timestamped photos of everything. Most landlords back down when they realize the tenant has organized documentation and knows the local rules. The scariest tenant to a bad landlord is not the angry one — it is the one with a folder.
