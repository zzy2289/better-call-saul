# Debt Collection Disputes

## Common dispute types

- Debt collector contacting about a debt you do not owe.
- Debt amount is incorrect.
- Debt is past the statute of limitations.
- Harassment by debt collectors (excessive calls, threats, public shaming).
- Collection on a debt already paid.
- Medical debt billing errors.
- Identity theft — debt belongs to someone else.
- Collector refuses to validate the debt.

## Core question

Is the debt valid, is the amount correct, and is the collector following the law in their collection practices?

## Key laws

### US — Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA)

- Collectors must identify themselves and the debt.
- You have 30 days to dispute the debt in writing after first contact.
- After dispute, collector must stop collection until they verify the debt.
- Collectors cannot: call before 8am or after 9pm, threaten violence, use obscene language, call your workplace if you ask them to stop, misrepresent the debt amount or legal status.
- Violations: statutory damages up to $1,000 per case plus actual damages and attorney fees.

### EU — Consumer Credit Directive and national laws

- Debt collection practices regulated at the national level.
- Many countries require written notice before collection activity.
- Right to dispute and request verification.

### China (民法典 / 治安管理处罚法)

- Violent or threatening collection is illegal.
- Collectors cannot harass family members or contacts.
- Online lending platform collection practices regulated by CBIRC guidelines.
- Excessive calls or threats can be reported to police (报警) or banking regulator (银保监会).

## Evidence checklist

- All correspondence from the collector.
- Original creditor information.
- Your payment records.
- Credit report showing the debt.
- Dates of all contact attempts.
- Recordings of calls (if legal in your jurisdiction — check one-party vs. two-party consent).
- Written dispute letter copies.
- Validation response (or lack thereof).

## Escalation ladder

1. Send a written debt validation request within 30 days of first contact.
2. If debt is invalid or expired, send a cease-and-desist letter.
3. File a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) in the US.
4. File a complaint with your state attorney general.
5. Report violations to the Federal Trade Commission (FTC).
6. Consult a consumer rights attorney (many work on contingency for FDCPA cases).
7. File a lawsuit for FDCPA violations.

## Strong angles

### Debt validation request

```text
This letter is in response to your contact on [date] regarding an alleged debt. Under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, I am requesting validation of this debt. Please provide:
1. The name of the original creditor.
2. The amount owed, itemized.
3. Proof that you are authorized to collect this debt.
4. Proof that the debt is within the statute of limitations.

Until this debt is validated, cease all collection activity.
```

### Statute of limitations defense

```text
The debt you are attempting to collect is past the statute of limitations in [state/jurisdiction], which is [X] years for this type of debt. The last activity on this account was [date]. You are not legally entitled to file suit on this debt. Continuing to attempt collection on a time-barred debt without disclosing its status may violate the FDCPA.
```

### Harassment complaint

```text
Your collection practices have included [specific violations: calls at prohibited hours, threats, excessive contact frequency, contacting third parties]. These practices violate the FDCPA Section [X]. I have documented [X] calls/contacts. Cease all telephone contact. Future communications must be in writing only. I am filing a complaint with the CFPB and reserve my right to seek statutory damages.
```

## Saul angle

Debt collectors rely on fear and urgency. Most people panic and either pay or hide. The smart move is to slow everything down. Ask for validation in writing. Check the statute of limitations. Read the FDCPA — it is one of the most consumer-friendly laws in existence, and collectors violate it constantly because most people do not know their rights. Every violation is money in your pocket if you document it properly.
