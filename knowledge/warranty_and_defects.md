# Warranty and Product Defect Disputes

## Common dispute types

- Manufacturer denies warranty claim.
- Seller says warranty expired but product failed prematurely.
- Warranty repair returns same defect.
- Extended warranty provider refuses coverage.
- Hidden defect discovered after warranty period.
- Product recall and insufficient remedy.

## Core question

Was the product defective at the time of sale or within a reasonable lifespan, and does the warranty or consumer protection law cover the issue?

## Key concepts

### Express warranty

A specific promise made by the manufacturer or seller about the product. This includes written warranty cards, advertising claims, and verbal promises (harder to prove).

### Implied warranty of merchantability

The product should work as a reasonable buyer would expect. A toaster should toast. A waterproof jacket should resist water. This exists in most jurisdictions even without a written warranty.

### Lemon laws

Some jurisdictions have specific laws for vehicles and major purchases that fail repeatedly despite repair attempts.

### EU Consumer Guarantee

- 2-year minimum legal guarantee for all consumer goods (not dependent on manufacturer warranty).
- Defect appearing within the first year is presumed to have existed at the time of purchase (burden of proof on seller).

### China Three Guarantees (三包)

- 7-day return, 15-day exchange, warranty period repair (product-category dependent).
- Smartphones/electronics: typically 1-year repair warranty.
- If repaired twice for the same defect and still failing: consumer may demand replacement.

## Evidence checklist

- Proof of purchase (receipt, order confirmation).
- Warranty documentation.
- Photos/video of the defect.
- Repair history and records.
- Communication with manufacturer or seller.
- Independent assessment or repair estimate if available.
- Product serial number and model.

## Escalation ladder

1. Contact seller/manufacturer with warranty claim and evidence.
2. If denied, request the specific warranty clause they are relying on.
3. Escalate within the company (supervisor, executive office).
4. File a complaint with consumer protection agency.
5. Social media escalation (factual, measured).
6. Small claims court.

## Strong angles

### Premature failure

```text
This product failed after [X months/years] of normal use. A reasonable consumer would expect a [product type] to last longer than [duration]. Even if the manufacturer's warranty has expired, the implied warranty of merchantability covers products that fail prematurely under normal use conditions.
```

### Repeated repair failure

```text
This product has been repaired [X] times for the same defect ([dates and details]). Despite multiple repair attempts, the defect persists. I am requesting a replacement or refund, as continued repair is clearly not resolving the issue.
```

### Warranty exclusion challenge

```text
Your denial cites [exclusion reason], but the defect is unrelated to the excluded condition. The [specific defect] is a manufacturing/design issue, not the result of [claimed misuse]. Please review the claim with this distinction in mind.
```

## Saul angle

Warranty denials often cite vague "misuse" or "normal wear" without evidence. Ask them to explain exactly how your use caused the defect. If they cannot, the denial is weak. Remember: the implied warranty exists separately from the manufacturer's written warranty, and it often lasts longer. Most companies would rather replace a product than defend a weak denial in front of a consumer protection agency.
