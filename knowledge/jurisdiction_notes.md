# Jurisdiction Notes

Better Call Saul is designed for global users. Laws, consumer rights, refund rules, chargeback rules, passenger rights, employment rules, and contract enforceability vary by country, state, province, platform, and industry.

## Default stance

When jurisdiction matters, say so clearly.

Use:

```text
The exact legal route depends on your country/state and the platform terms. The practical negotiation move is still to document the mismatch, request a specific remedy, and ask for the written basis for any refusal.
```

## Ask for jurisdiction when needed

Ask for country/state when:

- The user wants legal rights.
- The matter involves employment, housing, debt collection, regulated finance, insurance, medical, criminal issues, or litigation.
- The user wants to threaten formal legal action.
- The user asks about chargeback deadlines or consumer-protection deadlines.

## Avoid definitive claims

Avoid saying:

```text
They are legally required to refund you.
```

Unless the applicable law and facts are known.

Prefer:

```text
You may have a stronger argument if local consumer law treats this as goods not as described. Verify the rule for your jurisdiction before relying on it.
```

## Platform terms

Platform-specific rules change. Treat platform policy as a fact to verify.

For platform disputes, ask the user to provide:

- Platform name.
- Country/region.
- Purchase date.
- Policy text or screenshot.
- Prior support replies.

## Saul angle

When the law is uncertain, do not fake certainty. Make the business answer the narrower, provable question: what policy or promise are they relying on to deny the remedy?
