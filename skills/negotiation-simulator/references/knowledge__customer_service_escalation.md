# Customer Service Escalation

## Goal

Turn a support interaction from a vague complaint into a documented business problem that is easier to resolve than ignore.

## First-line support strategy

First-line support often follows scripts. The goal is not to win a debate with the first person. The goal is to create a clean record and reach the person or process that can approve the remedy.

## Evidence packet

Ask the user for or infer these items:

- Account/order number.
- Date of purchase or service issue.
- What was promised.
- What happened instead.
- Screenshots/photos.
- Prior ticket numbers.
- Names or chat transcripts if available.
- Desired remedy.

## Message structure

Use this structure:

1. One-sentence issue.
2. Short timeline.
3. Evidence list.
4. Clear remedy requested.
5. Deadline or next step.
6. Polite close.

Template:

```text
Hi, I am contacting you about [issue].

Timeline:
- [Date]: [what happened]
- [Date]: [what support said]
- [Date]: [current problem]

Attached evidence:
- [screenshot/photo/receipt]
- [ticket/chat/order reference]

I am requesting [specific remedy]. Please confirm by [reasonable date]. If this team cannot approve that, please escalate this to a supervisor or the team that handles [refunds/billing/disputes].
```

## Supervisor request

Ask for escalation without insulting the first agent.

```text
I understand you may not have authority to approve this. Please escalate the case to a supervisor or specialist team that can review the evidence and make an exception or correction.
```

## When support refuses

Common refusal patterns and counters:

### "Policy does not allow it"

Counter:

- Ask for the exact policy section.
- Explain why the facts fall outside normal policy.
- Ask for an exception review.

```text
Please point me to the exact policy section you are relying on. My concern is that this is not a standard change-of-mind return; it is a mismatch between what was promised and what was delivered.
```

### "The window has closed"

Counter:

- Show when issue was discovered.
- Show prior attempts to contact.
- Argue delay was caused by support/process/hidden defect.

```text
The normal window is not the right measure here because I contacted support on [date] and the issue was not resolved. I am asking you to treat the original contact date as the relevant date.
```

### "We already investigated"

Counter:

- Ask what evidence was reviewed.
- Provide missing evidence.
- Request a written explanation.

```text
Please confirm what evidence was reviewed and why the attached documents do not change the outcome. I need a written explanation before I decide the next escalation path.
```

## Escalation channels

Potential next steps:

- Supervisor.
- Formal complaint department.
- Platform dispute center.
- Payment provider dispute, if the claim is legitimate.
- Regulator or ombudsman, depending on country and industry.
- Factual public review.

## Public review rule

A review should be factual, short, and provable.

Good:

```text
I ordered [product/service] on [date]. The listing promised [promise], but I received [actual]. Support declined to resolve it after ticket [number]. I am still seeking [remedy].
```

Bad:

```text
These criminals scammed me and everyone should destroy them.
```

## Saul angle

The move is to make your case boringly hard to deny: clean timeline, evidence, reasonable ask, next escalation step.
