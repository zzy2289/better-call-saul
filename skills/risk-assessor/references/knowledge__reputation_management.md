# Reputation Management and Bad Reviews

## Goals

- De-escalate public conflict.
- Show future readers that the business is reasonable.
- Move sensitive details to a private channel.
- Offer a path to resolution.
- Avoid admitting legal liability unnecessarily.

## Public reply structure

1. Acknowledge frustration.
2. State the business cares about the issue.
3. Avoid arguing every detail publicly.
4. Invite direct contact.
5. Mention a specific next step.

Template:

```text
Thank you for sharing this. I am sorry the experience fell short of what you expected. We would like to review the details and see what can be done. Please contact [channel] with [order/reference], and we will look into it promptly.
```

## When the review is false

Do not explode publicly.

```text
We cannot match this account to any customer record based on the details provided, but we take concerns seriously. Please contact [channel] with the order or visit details so we can investigate.
```

## When the business made a mistake

```text
You are right that this should have been handled better. We have contacted you directly to make it right and are reviewing our process so this does not repeat.
```

## Avoid

- Calling the reviewer a liar.
- Revealing private customer information.
- Threatening legal action publicly.
- Long defensive essays.
- Copy-paste replies that ignore the actual complaint.

## Saul angle

A public reply is not mainly for the angry reviewer. It is for the next hundred people reading silently. Sound like the adult in the room.
